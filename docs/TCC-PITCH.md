# The CordCut Co-op™ (TCC)

## Cut the cord for good.

You're paying $50–70 a month for Netflix, Spotify, Disney+, and the rest.
Every year that's $600–800 — for content you don't own, that can vanish
overnight, with ads creeping in and prices climbing.

**What if it cost less — and you owned the library?**

The CordCut Co-op is a community-run media network. One shared library of
movies, music, TV, and audiobooks — built from the collections we already
own — streamed privately to any device, anywhere in the world.

**The name says it all:**

- **"CordCut"** is the promise — freedom from subscriptions.
- **"Co-op"** is the method — we own it together. No customers, no
  corporation. Just us.

---

## How it works

1. **Your media stays yours.** Every member runs their own piece of the
   co-op, on their own computer, with their own collection — the movies,
   music, and shows you already own. You manage your machine, your
   storage, your library. Nobody's the customer; nobody's the server farm.
2. **We connect our stores, privately.** The co-op links everyone's
   machines together over a private, encrypted network — like we all
   share one invisible router. No ports opened to the internet, nothing
   public, nothing tracked. Just us, from anywhere on earth.
3. **You choose who sees what.** Want a friend to browse your movie
   shelf? Approve them, pick exactly which folders they can see — that's
   it. Sharing is person-by-person and folder-by-folder, always. Your
   private stuff stays private, always.
4. **Everyone streams free.** Walk into the co-op's library from your
   phone, your TV, your laptop — anywhere, any network. No ads, no
   tracking, no "this title has left the library."

**The cost right now: $0 a month. That's it.**

The software is free. You run it on the computer you already own, over
the internet you already pay for. If the co-op ever grows big enough that
we want shared always-on hardware together, we'll decide that together —
as co-owners, each chipping in equally. Until then, everyone carries
their own, and nobody owes anybody anything.

## Home Binger — the co-op's storefront

Here's the part that makes TCC different from every other media server
you've seen: **you don't browse a wall of thumbnails. You walk in.**

**[Home Binger](https://github.com/BabyBluJ-wall/Home-Binger)** is the
face of the CordCut Co-op — a video store you visit. A real, walkable 3D
storefront:

- **Shelves stocked by the co-op** — your friends' collections displayed
  as actual movie cases you pick up and flip over, next to yours, next to
  the free public-domain archive.
- **A theater** — carry a case in, dim the lights, play it on the big
  screen with real spatial sound.
- **A jukebox and a DJ booth** — for the music side of the library.
- **Your phone is a visitor** — no app to install; any browser walks
  right in.

TCC is the co-op — the people, the rules, the shared library. **Home
Binger is the place it lives.** It's free, it's tiny, it runs on anything
Windows (and anything else with a browser), and it can even live on a USB
stick. If you can unzip a folder, you can join.

*(Already run Plex or Jellyfin? Plug it straight in — your existing

## What you get

- **The full co-op library** — every movie, album, show, and audiobook
  the members have shared, on any device, from anywhere in the world.
- **A private, encrypted connection** — the co-op's network is closed and
  invisible to the public internet.
- **Total control of your own stuff** — approve each friend, share each
  folder, revoke anytime. What you don't share doesn't exist to anyone
  else.
- **A voice in the co-op** — this is *ours*, not a company's. Every big
  decision — like adding shared hardware — gets decided together.
- **A video store, for crying out loud** — you and your friends' entire
  collections, on the shelves of a store you can walk around in. Come on.

## Why this beats what you have now

- **Save $600–800 every year.** $0 instead of $50–70 a month.
- **Ownership.** The library is ours. No corporation can delete a movie
  or pull a song.
- **Privacy.** No one tracks what you watch or listen to — the software
  collects nothing at all.
- **It grows with you.** Bring your collection — every new member makes
  the store better for everyone.
- **Community, not subscription.** You're not a customer. You're a
  co-owner.

## Your media, protected

- **Your collection, digitized your way.** The co-op provides a plain
  guide for turning your CDs and DVDs into files that live on your own
  machine — plus backup recommendations so a dead drive never takes your
  library with it.
- **Built to stay ours.** Nothing sits on someone else's cloud, no
  company can switch us off, and the whole network runs member-to-member
  with no middleman.
- **The long-term dream** — if the co-op grows into it: a shared
  always-on archive with redundant drives, and eventually solar + battery
  backup, so the library outlasts any hard drive, any power outage, and
  any corporation's decision to delete what we paid for. *That's a
  "we'll build it together" future — not a launch promise.*

## The honest fine print (we're doing this right)

- This is for media each member **legally owns** — your purchased DVDs,
  CDs, and digital buys. We digitize our own collections.
- **We only share within the co-op** — a private, invitation-only group
  of friends sharing what they own, the digital equivalent of lending
  each other movies and albums. Never public, never sold.
- **No money moves** — the software is free, and every member covers
  their own machine. If shared hardware ever happens, it'll be equal
  contributions for infrastructure only — like chipping in for gas on a
  road trip. Never for content.
- **Free, legal, open software** — built on Home Binger and standard
  tools anyone can inspect.
- The lines we will not cross: downloading media we don't own, opening
  the library to the public, or charging anyone for content. Those are
  the things that actually get people in trouble — and they're the
  opposite of what a co-op is.
